[SLIDE 16 — Potential Risks and Challenges]

Despite the potential opportunities in Poland, Premier Inn would also face several important risks.

First, there are economic risks. Inflation, rising wages and exchange-rate fluctuations could increase operating costs and reduce profitability.

Second, competition is a major challenge. Poland already has established international hotel chains and strong local competitors. Premier Inn cannot assume that its success in the UK will automatically be repeated in Poland.

Another important issue is cultural differences. Language barriers, management styles and different customer expectations could create misunderstandings.

There are also political and legal challenges. Premier Inn would need to comply with Polish employment laws, taxation requirements and relevant European Union regulations. Brexit adds further complexity to cross-border business operations.

Finally, there are operational and financial risks. Staffing, suppliers and service quality all need to be managed carefully. Long-term hotel leases could also become a financial burden if occupancy levels are lower than expected.

However, these risks could be reduced through local partnerships, careful financial planning and a gradual market entry.

[SLIDE 17 — Recommendations and Strategies]

So, how should Premier Inn approach these challenges? We have identified six key recommendations.

First, cultural adaptation. Premier Inn should maintain its British brand identity but adapt its services and communication to Polish customers. This is known as glocalisation — thinking globally but acting locally.

Second, local partnerships. Working with Polish small and medium-sized businesses, suppliers and property specialists would provide valuable local knowledge.

Third, our recommended market entry strategy is phased hotel leasing. This would allow Premier Inn to enter the market without immediately purchasing expensive properties, while maintaining control over its hotel operations.

Fourth, employee development. The company should recruit Polish managers and provide cross-cultural training to improve communication and customer service.

Fifth, regulatory compliance. Local legal advisers should help Premier Inn understand Polish employment, taxation and European regulations.

Finally, financial risk management is essential. The company should monitor operating costs, set competitive room prices and regularly review financial performance.

Together, these recommendations would give Premier Inn a more controlled and realistic opportunity to enter Poland.

[SLIDE 18 — Conclusion]

To conclude, we believe that Poland represents a promising opportunity for Premier Inn, but expansion should be approached with caution.

Our analysis shows that Poland has a growing economy and increasing tourism demand. This creates opportunities for affordable accommodation and potential long-term business growth.

However, our research also highlights important challenges, particularly strong competition, financial uncertainty, cultural differences and regulatory requirements.

We therefore recommend starting with one pilot hotel, potentially in Warsaw, Kraków or Gdańsk, using a leasing strategy supported by local Polish partners.

Premier Inn should also maintain its British identity while adapting its services to local customer expectations.

One important lesson from Germany is that increased revenue does not automatically mean increased profit. This is why financial performance must be carefully monitored before further investment.

Our final recommendation is simple: proceed with caution, test the market first, and only expand when the business model has demonstrated that it can be profitable.
